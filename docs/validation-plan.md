# Does LearnLoop improve delayed recall?

## 0.3 measurement guardrails

Gap matching and ordering provided steps are supported extra practice. Their events explicitly use extra_practice_not_scheduled_recall and do not alter FSRS, repeat gates, daily introductions, or the delayed-success counter. Log their exposure/time separately when analyzing the full backup; completion is not a recall outcome.

The three optional examples derive from DCF-09, DCF-14 and DCF-18. Do not place their underlying concepts in the control condition while practicing the examples; either omit the examples for this comparison or keep all overlapping concepts in the intervention condition and match distinct concepts appropriately. Prefer a fresh small subject set if prior exposure makes the original 24 unsuitable.

Keep pace settings and guided/recall-first choice fixed during a comparison and record them. Test independent unfamiliar wording/scenarios without gaps, choices or provided process steps. Assess content clarity/source support and interface friction separately from memory. The 28 passing software tests and browser walkthrough do not demonstrate learning benefit.


This prototype records practice, not proof of effectiveness. Choice accuracy, repetition count and session completion are process measures. Learning is the ability to explain and apply an idea after time has passed without the app's cues.

## Small personal crossover trial

1. Review the 24 items for clarity and source support before starting. Pair concepts of similar difficulty and split each pair randomly: half LearnLoop practice, half ordinary reading/review. Save the assignment and baseline results outside normal app practice history. Avoid putting the exact same fact in both conditions.
2. Take a brief baseline free-response test on parallel scenarios. Self-mark against 1–3 predetermined required ideas, ideally having another person mark anonymized answers with the condition hidden. Use unfamiliar wording/context rather than the practice prompt verbatim.
3. Spend equal total study time over seven days, e.g. five minutes in each condition on scheduled study days. LearnLoop includes review/choice/recall; control uses rereading/reviewing the same kind of notes. Log minutes, extra outside exposure and any source corrections. Don't practice the control items through LearnLoop during the comparison.
4. Test without choices, notes or feedback at seven days, and preferably again after 21 days. Counterbalance two equivalent test forms. Count required ideas recalled, complete responses and defensible application to a new situation. Record time and confidence if helpful, without conflating them with accuracy.
5. Compare percentage of required ideas retrieved in each condition, change from baseline, complete-response rate, time per idea retained, and Sam's friction/return preference. Also inspect the mistakes: missed dependency, confused role, weak system connection, or wording ambiguity.

## Practical decision rule

Before the trial, choose a useful personal threshold, for example at least a 15 percentage-point gain in seven-day idea recall at similar study time, without sessions feeling burdensome. With 24 items and one learner this is directional evidence, not a causal or generalizable efficacy claim. If the result is unclear, improve ambiguous items or the workflow and repeat with a fresh small subject set; do not train scheduler parameters on this history.

Recognition and production events stay separate. Exclude conflicted/pending attempts and content-reset revisions from comparable recall outcomes. The Today delayed-success count only identifies successful attempts at least 24 hours after a previous recall attempt; it does not control exposure or supply a randomized baseline. Use the full JSON export for analysis, not that count alone.

## Separate quality checks

**Content:** source supports the answer, scope/date/context retained, rubric admits equivalent wording, choice has one defensible best answer, distractors reflect real misconceptions without accidental cues, scenario tests flow rather than memorized project details.

**Client:** Sam can finish a session on the phone without explanation, use the reveal/checklist honestly, understand next return/pending status, correct a misleading item, capture a useful note in under two minutes, and save/restore a backup. Track abandonment and input friction independently of content scores.

A small friend pilot can help usability and content review after access is approved. Each learner's progress stays separate; do not use a shared anonymous progress account to compare recall.
